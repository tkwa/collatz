# The Weakest Kakeya Improvement

Let

```math
h_0=3+\frac{\sqrt{17665}-97}{600}.
```

and

```math
m_0=\frac{159+\sqrt{145}}{56}.
```

The conjecture is the following disjunction:

1. every Kakeya set $K\subset\mathbb R^4$ has upper Minkowski dimension
   $\overline{\dim}_{M}K\gt h_0$; or
2. there is an $\eta\gt0$ such that the four-dimensional Kakeya maximal
   estimate holds at dimension $m_0+\eta$; or
3. there is one fixed finite $q\ge3$ such that $A_3(q)$ holds, with the notation
   of the [folder README](README.md).

The first alternative is weaker than a uniform numerical improvement: the
gap above $h_0$ may depend on $K$, and upper Minkowski dimension is weaker than
lower Minkowski or Hausdorff dimension.  The second asks for any strict
improvement over the best published four-dimensional maximal exponent.  The
third asks that the density loss in the 2025 three-dimensional shaded-tube
theorem be bounded by one exponent independent of $\varepsilon$; it does not
ask for the sharp exponent $3$.

This is the folder's unique moving **Weakest** statement.  Its openness
qualifications and the rejected weakenings are recorded in the
[source audit](dev_notes/sources_and_implications.md#weakening-audit).
